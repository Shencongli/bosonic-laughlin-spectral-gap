EXPLICIT UNIFORM FLUX THRESHOLD FOR THE BOSONIC V0 NEGATIVE TAIL

Result. With the definitions and normalization of Section 3 of
outputs/bosonic_V0_tail_estimate.tex, every integer Q >= 10,000,000 obeys

  T_{9,Q} <= (c_9 + 1/2000) (S_{4,Q} + H_Q),
  c_9 = 30(5/9)^9 = 19531250/129140163.                 (1)

This is an exact operator/form bound, uniform in bosonic particle number.
It uses the exact auxiliary eigenvalue formula and coherent lifting formula
already proved in that section. The scalar estimates below are explicit for
every Q in the stated range, not merely at a sampled value of Q. No floating
point comparison occurs in the proof or its three finite rational checks.

DEFINITIONS AND THE TWO INPUT IDENTITIES

Set a=2/27, M=floor(aQ), r=9/10, t=9/29 and rho=29/36.
On the auxiliary pair--single space V_Q tensor U_Q, let Pi_z be the
orthogonal projection onto total spin 3Q/2-z, for 0<=z<=Q. Let

  f_{z,Q} = (Q)_z/(2Q)_z,
  C_{9,Q} = 2 sum_{z odd, 9<=z<=Q} f_{z,Q} Pi_z.

Here (x)_k is the descending factorial. The positive coherent auxiliary
operator A_{M,Q} of Section 3 has eigenvalues tau_{z,Q}>0 satisfying

  tau_{z,Q} = [(2Q+1)/(3Q-2z+1)] sum_{j=z}^Q b_{j,Q} gamma_{z,j,Q},
  b_{j,Q} = [(Q+1)/(M+Q+1)] (Q)_j/(M+Q)_j,
  gamma_{z,j,Q} = [(2Q)_z/(3Q-z+1)_z]
                  binom(j,z) (Q-z)_{j-z}/(3Q-2z)_{j-z}.             (2)

All summands in (2) are nonnegative. The coherent frame identity and the
occupation estimate on the output sector after a pair annihilation give

  L_3(W_3 A_{M,Q} W_3^dagger)
        <= [(Q+1)/(M+1)] (S_{4,Q}+H_Q).                            (3)

The linear map X -> L_3(W_3 X W_3^dagger) is positive. Thus an auxiliary
bound C_{9,Q}<=k A_{M,Q} can be inserted into (3) without changing k.

STEP 1: AN EXACT LOWER BOUND ON EVERY RETAINED SUMMAND

For integers 0<=z<=j<=100 and Q>=100, define the limiting summand

  ell_{z,j} := (2/3)^(z+1) (1+a)^(-j-1)
                       binom(j,z) (1/3)^(j-z)
              = 2^(z+1) binom(j,z) t^(j+1).                       (4)

No limit is taken in using ell; it is an explicitly defined positive
rational number. Bounds on each factor of (2) show that its j-th summand
is at least ell_{z,j} times the following product:

  P_{z,j,Q} = product_{i=0}^{j-1} (1-i/Q)
             product_{i=0}^{z-1} (1-i/(2Q))
             product_{i=z}^{j-1} (1-i/Q).                         (5)

Indeed, (2Q+1)/(3Q-2z+1)>=2/3 and
(Q+1)/(M+Q+1)>=1/(1+a). Each factor of (Q)_j/(M+Q)_j is at least
(1-i/Q)/(1+a), since M+Q-i <= (1+a)Q. Each factor of
(2Q)_z/(3Q-z+1)_z is at least (2/3)(1-i/(2Q)), since
3Q-z+1-i<=3Q whenever z>=1; for z=0 that product is empty. Finally,
each factor of (Q-z)_{j-z}/(3Q-2z)_{j-z} is at least
(1/3)(1-(z+i)/Q), because its denominator is at most 3Q.

Every loss factor appearing in (5) lies in [0,1]. The elementary product
inequality product_k(1-u_k)>=1-sum_k u_k for 0<=u_k<=1 (proved by induction)
therefore gives

  P_{z,j,Q} >= 1 - [j(j-1)-z(z-1)/4]/Q
             >= 1 - 9900/Q.                                      (6)

Retaining j<=100 in the nonnegative sum (2), for 0<=z<=32 one obtains

  tau_{z,Q} >= (1-9900/Q) sum_{j=z}^{100} ell_{z,j}.                 (7)

STEP 2: A UNIFORM EXACT BOUND ON THE OMITTED LIMITING SUM

The binomial-series identity, valid since 0<t<1, implies

  sum_{j=z}^infinity ell_{z,j}
      = [2t/(1-t)]^(z+1) = r^(z+1).                               (8)

For j>=0, binom(j,z)<=2^j follows by retaining one term of
sum_{k=0}^j binom(j,k)=2^j (with binom(j,z)=0 when z>j).
Since 2t=18/29<1, for every 0<=z<=32 the relative omitted tail obeys

  [sum_{j=101}^infinity ell_{z,j}]/r^(z+1)
     <= (2/r)^(z+1) [t/(1-2t)] (2t)^101
     <= (20/9)^33 (9/11) (18/29)^101
     <= 1/1000.                                                   (9)

The last comparison is the first of three explicit rational checks below.
For Q>=10^7, 9900/Q<=1/1000, so (7)--(9) yield

  tau_{z,Q} >= (999/1000)^2 r^(z+1)
             >= (499/500) r^(z+1),   0<=z<=32.                    (10)

Moreover, (Q-i)/(2Q-i)<=1/2 for 0<=i<z<=Q, hence
f_{z,Q}<=2^(-z). Define the positive scalar

  A_* := (2/r)(1/(2r))^9 = (20/9)(5/9)^9 = a c_9.                 (11)

Because 1/(2r)=5/9<1, (10) gives

  2 f_{z,Q}/tau_{z,Q} <= (500/499) A_*,  9<=z<=32.                (12)

The estimate covers all these integer z; only the odd ones are needed.

STEP 3: THE UNBOUNDED z RANGE

For completeness, retaining just j=z in (2) gives the previously proved
coarse bound

  2 f_{z,Q}/tau_{z,Q} <= 3(1+a) rho^z,
  rho = 3(1+a)/4 = 29/36,                  0<=z<=Q.               (13)

It follows directly from the exact product

  2 [(3Q-2z+1)/(2Q+1)] [(M+Q+1)/(Q+1)]
      product_{i=0}^{z-1}
       [(M+Q-i)(3Q-z+1-i)/(2Q-i)^2]

as an upper bound on 2f/tau. Its prefactor is at most 3(1+a).
Writing x=i/Q, each product factor is at most
(1+a-x)(3-2x)/(2-x)^2 <=3(1+a)/4. The latter inequality holds because
its denominator is positive and its numerator difference is
x[(8-4a)-(5-3a)x]/4>=0 for 0<=x<1 and 0<a<1/3.

The second exact rational check is

  3(29/27)(29/36)^33 <= A_*.                                     (14)

Since 0<rho<1, (13)--(14) imply 2f/tau<=A_* for all z>=33,
uniformly in Q. Combining with (12), and observing that the left
side vanishes on the other auxiliary spin blocks, gives

  C_{9,Q} <= (500/499) A_* A_{M,Q},   Q>=10^7.                    (15)

STEP 4: LIFTING AND THE FINAL CONSTANT

Because M+1>aQ,

  (Q+1)/(M+1) <= (1/a)(1+1/Q) <= (1/a)(1001/1000),               (16)

where the last inequality only requires Q>=1000. Applying (3) and (15)
then proves

  T_{9,Q} <= (1001/998)c_9 (S_{4,Q}+H_Q)
           <= (c_9+1/2000)(S_{4,Q}+H_Q).                          (17)

For the final rational check, the difference between the two coefficients
in the second inequality is strictly positive:

  1/2000 - (3/998)c_9
    = 1,949,063,779 / 42,960,627,558,000 > 0.                    (18)

The margin is about 4.54e-5, but this decimal is not used. The only uniform
Q conditions used above are Q>=100, Q>=9,900,000 (to make 9900/Q<=1/1000),
and Q>=1000. Hence 10,000,000 is a convenient valid threshold; the slightly
smaller integer 9,900,000 is also valid by the identical argument.
