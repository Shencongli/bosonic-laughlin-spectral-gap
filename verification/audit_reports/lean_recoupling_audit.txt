Object and normalization checked
The auxiliary space is exactly ThreeBodyAux Q = Fin(2Q+1) -> Fin(Q+1) -> C.
Its two factors are the normalized maximal pair-spin-Q representation and the
one-particle spin-Q/2 representation, with integer flux Q >= 0. The indices p,k
are orbital deficits, so the total twice-magnetic-weight operator is
H phi(p,k) = (3Q - 2(p+k)) phi(p,k). The square-root ladder coefficients are
sqrt((i+1)(D-i)) for a spin-D/2 factor; raising lowers the deficit. The proved
commutators [E,F]=H, [H,E]=2E, [H,F]=-2F use this convention consistently.

The insertion uses the actual pairVector kernel already used by the V0
Hamiltonian, not an abstract representation with an assumed identification.
Its entries are delta_(x+y,p) sqrt(binomial(Q,x) binomial(Q,y)/binomial(2Q,p)).
The new threeBodyContract is J-adjoint on the full tensor space and has no
sqrt(3) factor. It is therefore distinct from the earlier threeBodyAdjoint,
which is sqrt(3) J-adjoint on physical symmetric three-particle states.
Consequently compressedSwap is exactly J-adjoint (23) J, and the earlier
W3-adjoint W3 = I+2T normalization is unchanged. No fermionic exchange sign or
particle-number-dependent interaction normalization was introduced.

Equivariance checked
PairLadder proves the binomial square-root ladder identities with their
endpoint cases. PairEquivariance proves both raising and lowering insertion
intertwining directly for this kernel. PairContractionEquivariance obtains
the corresponding contraction identities by finite bilinear transposition.
Using a bilinear identity here is intentional: all kernel/ladder coefficients
are real. The separate angular adjoint theorem includes complex conjugation.
CompressedSwapEquivariance and CompressedSwapLower prove actual T E=E T,
T F=F T, and T H=H T for every natural Q and every auxiliary vector. They
have no equivariance hypothesis. The all-Q statements include Q=0; no
nonexistent z=1 block is asserted at Q=0.

Highest-weight classification checked
The vector auxHighestWeight Q z is supported on p+k=z, is normalized by its
p=0 coefficient being one (it is not declared to have unit Hilbert norm),
and is nonzero and killed by E for 0 <= z <= Q. The proof establishes that
an E-kernel vector in one deficit block is determined by that coefficient,
and that no nonzero E-kernel vector has deficit z>Q. This excludes additional
highest-weight sectors, rather than merely checking proposed vectors.
Endpoint extraction uses the actual compressedSwap sum and the previously
proved falling-factorial identity. Kernel uniqueness then upgrades that
single component to the full eigenvector equation.

Remaining hypotheses in intermediate statements
HighestWeight.auxHighestWeight_swap_of_raise_commutes and the two named
SwapEigenvalues '_of_commutation' lemmas explicitly take a commutation
hypothesis. SwapSpectrum supplies compressedSwap_auxRaise to each of them.
No such hypothesis is left in compressedSwap_highestWeight,
compressedSwap_eigenvalue, compressedSwap_spectrum, or
compressedSwap_complete_labeled_basis. Their quantifiers concern the actual
defined operator; there is no freely chosen surrogate Hamiltonian or T.

Spectral completeness checked
compressedSwap_spectrum proves equality of sets of complex spectral values:
  spectrum(T_Q) = { (-1)^z (Q)_z/(2Q)_z : 0 <= z <= Q }.
Both directions are present: every value has a concrete nonzero highest
vector, and every eigenvalue is reduced to one by projecting to a nonzero
deficit block and successively applying raising until its kernel is reached.
The finite-dimensional spectrum/eigenvector equivalence is proved for the
matrix of this same map.

SwapDiagonalization applies the finite Hermitian spectral theorem to the
actual compressedSwapMatrix. It provides a complete orthonormal eigenbasis
in EuclideanSpace C (Fin(2Q+1) x Fin(Q+1)). auxEuclideanEquiv explicitly curries
coordinates into ThreeBodyAux; it is declared as a linear equivalence, not
as an isometry. compressedSwapEigenbasis is correspondingly a Basis on the
curried auxiliary type. The coordinate inner product agrees with the
physical finite tensor inner product. The complete_labeled_basis theorem
labels each vector in this fixed basis by one of the explicit recoupling
eigenvalues and proves reconstruction of every auxiliary vector.

Multiplet dimensions and the distinction from spectral-set completeness
SwapSpectrum alone gives the full spectral set and a complete eigenbasis;
neither statement alone would imply a specified multiplicity. The additional
MultipletDimensions theorem supplies a separate proof that the eigenspace
of (-1)^z (Q)_z/(2Q)_z has dimension exactly 3Q-2z+1 for 0 <= z <= Q.
Specifically, its vectors are the actual lowering descendants F^n phi_z for
0 <= n <= 3Q-2z. AngularMultiplets proves the raising coefficient
E F^(n+1) phi_z = (n+1)(3Q-2z-n) F^n phi_z and the H weight 3Q-2z-2n.
These identities establish nonzeroness and linear independence in this range.
The proved T F=F T keeps them in the actual T eigenspace, giving its lower
dimension bound. RecouplingValues proves distinct labels have distinct
eigenvalues by strictly decreasing positive magnitudes. Thus the eigenspaces
are independent, so the sum of their dimensions is at most the ambient
(2Q+1)(Q+1). The exact natural-number sum of the lower bounds is this same
ambient dimension, forcing equality for every eigenspace. This argument
includes Q=0 and has no large-Q hypothesis or numerical rank test.

The twice-highest-weight 3Q-2z corresponds to the manuscript spin
J=3Q/2-z in the usual hbar=1 convention. This is supported by the actual
ladder operators and dimensions, not just by a matching dimension formula.
The formal objects use Lie-algebra operators, concrete descendants, and
eigenspaces; a named SU(2) group representation or a Casimir operator is not
introduced. The spin identification is realized through this explicit
normalized angular-momentum ladder.

Orthogonal projectors and return to the physical three-body coefficient
SwapProjectors groups the complete orthonormal matrix eigenbasis according
to the actual spectral value, then conjugates the diagonal indicator
matrices by its unitary eigenvector matrix. The theorems prove Hermitian
idempotence, pairwise orthogonality, sum Pi_z=I, and
T=sum_z (-1)^z f_z Pi_z. This is an actual operator resolution, not merely
a list of proposed eigenvalues. SpectralPartition's generic star-algebra
automorphism hypothesis is instantiated by this explicit unitary
conjugation. SwapProjectorEigenspaces proves that the curried linear map's
range is exactly the corresponding T eigenspace. SwapProjectorRanks then
proves rank(Pi_z)=3Q-2z+1, with rank explicitly defined as complex dimension
of that image.

ThreeBodySpectral defines the Gram matrix I+2T and proves that its action
equals the existing threeBodyAdjoint(threeBodyMap(phi)), so it does not
silently substitute a merely isospectral matrix. It proves the actual
Gram resolution with eigenvalues 1+2(-1)^z f_z and the physical N=3 bosonic
normal-order coefficient resolution
S3 psi = sum_z 2(-1)^z f_z W3 Pi_z W3-adjoint psi.
The latter retains the explicit IsBosonic psi hypothesis. It does not
claim the arbitrary-N lift. The full auxiliary Gram spectrum should also
not be identified indiscriminately with the full physical H3 spectrum:
the nonzero spectral values transfer through W3, whereas zero eigenspaces
can differ between its domain and codomain. The reviewed theorems respect
this distinction.

These are exact finite-dimensional operator/spectral results for all Q,
not numerical finite-Q extrapolations. The operator resolutions concern
the auxiliary exchange/Gram maps and the physical three-boson coefficient,
not the arbitrary-particle Hamiltonian. This audit does not certify the
positive lift to every particle sector, coherent-tail bounds, finite
certificates, or the final UniformGapTarget.
