Scope and checked proof structure

1. PhysicalComparisonForms.lean defines Phi^* A Phi using the actual linear
   map into ordered-tensor states. Its sesquilinear identity has arbitrary
   complex inputs. Composition is exact congruence. Highest-weight frames
   include WeightState into State explicitly, and range completeness follows
   from the previously proved linear equivalence, including degree zero.

2. NonorthogonalHamiltonian.lean derives the coordinate action G^{-1} H
   from range invariance and a proved inverse physical metric. Hamiltonian
   invariance uses bosonic and fixed-weight preservation and coordinate
   surjectivity. For Q>0, the actual square form is H G^{-1} H. The actual
   three-particle normal-order identity then gives H G^{-1} H-H. No external
   comparison array occurs in these physical equalities.

3. SparseGramCertificate.lean verifies S M=R^T diag(w) R over integers,
   including the checked transpose. Positive S and nonnegative weights imply
   complex PSD, and a positive denominator transports it to rational arrays.
   The sparse row identities do not assume unique or sorted entries. Three
   regression tests reject corrupted matrix/transpose data and check the
   necessity of the scalar/weight sign premises.

4. KernelComplement.lean transports exact kernel frames under nonzero
   matrix scaling, identifies matrices sharing a complete kernel frame,
   and reconstructs an entire Hermitian form from a complementary frame.
   Active positive definiteness uses full kernel reconstruction, L W=0,
   R W=I, PSD, and the library theorem x^* A x=0 iff A x=0. Orthogonality
   of W to the kernel is not required.

5. RetainedPlanarCertificates.lean checks 26 comparison arrays and their
   26 Hamiltonian arrays, using direct kernel reduction of integer witnesses.
   It proves complex PSD, complete common kernels, nullities, complementary
   coordinate identities, and active positive definiteness. The two degree-one
   blocks are empty; their positive-definiteness statements are vacuous.
   The largest block has dimension 10, rank 8, and nullity 2.
