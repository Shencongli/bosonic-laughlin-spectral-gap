Model and coordinate conventions
--------------------------------
The underlying Hamiltonian is the existing finite spherical LLL bosonic V0
model, with Q+1 orthonormal one-particle orbitals and coefficient one for every
unordered-pair projector. Orbital labels are deficits from the north-pole
orbital; total deficit is d. The ordered tensor-coordinate inner product is
conjugate-linear in its first argument. Physical vectors are permutation
invariant. This increment changes coordinates and proves the required maps;
it does not change the Hamiltonian normalization or take a continuum limit.

1. Actual occupation quotient and orbit count
--------------------------------------------
Occupation Q N is Mathlib's Sym (Orbital Q) N, namely multisets of actual
orbitals with cardinality N. The map from configurations uses List.ofFn
followed by the multiset quotient. Equality of multisets is proved equivalent
to a permutation of the particle slots. Consequently the unscaled map
I(c)(a)=c(occupation(a)) is injective, has bosonic range, and is onto the
bosonic tensor subspace. Its inverse is a chosen representative evaluation;
Bose symmetry makes that evaluation independent of the representative.

The fiber multiplicity m(A) is defined as the actual cardinality of the
ordered configurations with occupation A. The proof uses the actual
permutation orbit and Mathlib's stabilizer decomposition into permutations
of equal-label fibers. It proves
  m(A) * product_j n_j! = N!,
including N=0 and repeated labels. No orbit-size formula is assumed. The
sesquilinear inner product is correspondingly sum_A m(A) conjugate(c_A)e_A.

Weight restriction and zero extension have exact inverse/support properties.
The square relating occupation inclusion to ordered tensor weight inclusion
commutes. The weight occupation coordinate space is complete for the bosonic
weight subspace, not an additional low-energy truncation.

2. Relation to sorted tuples
---------------------------
Sorted orbital tuples are identified explicitly with WeightOccupation.
For d<=Q, every entry is at most its nonnegative total d, so the cap is
inactive and there is an explicit equivalence with nondecreasing natural
tuples of length N and sum d. These are precisely the mathematical indexing
constraints used by parts(n,D). The theorem does not certify the Python
enumeration recursion, its ordering, or any numerical output array. It
provides the coordinate correspondence needed to specify such arrays.

3. Factorial-scaled coefficients and actual pair maps
----------------------------------------------------
The polynomial occupation inclusion is
  chi(a) = f(A)c_A,  f(A)=product_j n_j!,  A=occupation(a).
Both the factorial scaling and the orbital binomial scaling are explicitly
invertible; neither is treated as a unitary change of basis.

The one-label occupation derivative has row coefficient n_x in its source.
The two-label derivative has coefficient n_x*(n_y-delta_xy). The code proves
this by sequential count/erase operations, so the coincident-label case
has the required falling factorial rather than n_x^2. The ordered sum x+y=p
contains both orders for unequal labels, exactly as the raw polynomial pair
channel does. There is no extra unmentioned factor of two.

The factorial-cons identities prove full operator intertwining with the
actual raw tensor pair channel. Restriction yields finite rectangular weight
matrices, their explicit integer entries, and actual zero-extension and
matrix-action identities. Channels p>d vanish before restriction; natural
subtraction d-p therefore introduces no artificial weight-zero output.
Completeness of the factorial occupation basis and the physical common-kernel
characterization are proved using the actual maps, rather than inferred
from a numerical rank or an apparent dimension match.

4. Exact finite-sphere metric and pair coefficients
--------------------------------------------------
Write D_Q(a)=product_i sqrt(binomial(Q,a_i)) and
  alpha(Q,N,d)=sqrt(Q^d/N!).
The final physical coordinate map is Phi=alpha D_Q^(-1) I_f, with I_f the
factorial occupation inclusion. Its injectivity and bosonic-weight
surjectivity require Q>0 where the sector scalar must be invertible.

OccupationPolynomialMetric derives the full complex inner product, not only
norms, by grouping the actual tensor sum into fibers and using the proved
identity m(A)f(A)=N!. Before multiplying by alpha^2, the diagonal coefficient
is N! f(A)/product_i binomial(Q,a_i).

SphereFactors and OccupationSphereMetric retain the exact finite products
  P_j(Q)=product_(0<=s<j)(1-s/Q).
For j<=Q and positive Q, j! binomial(Q,j)=Q^j P_j(Q), with P_j(Q)>0.
Thus the normalized fixed-degree metric has the finite-sphere form
  f(A) product_i a_i! / product_i P_(a_i)(Q).
The actual pair Gram coefficient becomes
  [p!/2^(p+1)] / P_p(2Q),
using p<=2Q, p<=d, and the explicit cancellation of the N-particle and
N+2-particle sector scalars with choose(N+2,2).

The script exact_relative_blocks.py defines the planar numerator metric
g_A=product_j n_j! (j!)^(n_j) and planar coefficient kappa_p^2=p!/2^(p+1).
At finite Q the P factors are retained. These identities are not asymptotic
expansions and do not identify the finite-sphere matrix with the planar
matrix obtained by setting those factors to one.

FockInner establishes H=sum_p B_p^dagger B_p as a full sesquilinear identity
on bosonic bra and ket vectors. PolynomialGram transports every matrix
element through the explicit nonunitary diagonal map. Consequently later
Gram matrices are tied to the actual Hamiltonian, with its original pair
normalization; a quadratic-form assertion is not silently promoted to a
different matrix representation.

5. Actual highest-weight condition
---------------------------------
TensorHighestWeight defines total J+ as the sum of the actual normalized
spin-Q/2 single-slot raising operators. It decreases the total deficit by
one, preserves Bose symmetry, and includes the cap boundary, zero-deficit,
zero-flux and vacuum cases. PolynomialHighestWeight proves the exact identity
  D_Q J+ = CM D_Q,
where each tensor coordinate derivative has coefficient j. Its full and
fixed-weight matrix kernels therefore represent the actual J+ kernel.
No claim about thermal, low-energy, or phase-level behavior is used here.

OccupationHighestWeight proves the integer coefficient j*n_j from the source
occupation by explicit count/erase replacement and the factorial metric
identity. OccupationCMBlocks supplies the actual matrix-action identity,
the inclusion intertwiner, and a linear equivalence between its full kernel
and the actual bosonic highest-weight subspace. A particular chosen nullspace
frame is not required for this complete-subspace result.

CertificateHighestWeight retains the normalization between adjacent degrees:
  J+ Phi_(N,d) = sqrt(Q) Phi_(N,d-1) CM_(N,d),  d>0.
For Q>0 it proves the corresponding matrix-kernel iff and surjectivity onto
the physical highest-weight vectors. The scalar sqrt(Q) is not discarded in
the operator identity. The unnormalized polynomial/occupation APIs include
Q=0; the normalized invertibility claims explicitly require positive Q.

There is a target-space convention at d=0: the Lean natural-number expression
d-1 is 0, so its CM block is the zero map from degree zero to degree zero.
The script parts(n,-1) is empty, so cm_lower(n,0) has an empty row index.
These maps have the same full input kernel, but their target dimensions and
literal matrices differ. The stated source-column rule and equal-entry
identification with the script's CM matrix use d>0 and the cap-inactive
regime d<=Q, together with the sorted-tuple index correspondence above.
The analogous p>d pair branch is explicitly proved zero before restriction.
