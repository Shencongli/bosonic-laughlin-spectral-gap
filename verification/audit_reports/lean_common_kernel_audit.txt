Scope and normalization
-----------------------
The model is the finite spherical LLL V0 Hamiltonian at integer flux Q, in
Q+1 orthonormal orbitals indexed by 0,...,Q. State Q M is the ordered tensor
coordinate space; IsBosonic specifies its physical symmetric subspace. The
inner product sums over all ordered configurations and is conjugate-linear in
its first argument. H is the sum over unordered particle pairs of normalized
pair projectors, each with coefficient one. No new rescaling of H is made.
The total orbital deficit d is the sum of the orbital indices, rather than a
particle number, a pair relative angular momentum, or a highest-weight label.

1. Actual Hamiltonian kernel and right-factored operators
--------------------------------------------------------
PhysicalKernel derives zero energy iff H psi=0 from the exact sum of squared
norms of distinct-slot pair projectors. This ambient statement needs no Bose
symmetry. On M=N+2 bosons it uses the previously proved normalization
  B_p = sqrt(choose(N+2,2)) times contraction with the normalized pair vector
to prove H psi=0 iff every B_p psi=0. Bose symmetry is explicitly required for
this first-two-slot contraction characterization. Without that hypothesis it
would not characterize the full ambient tensor kernel.

PairSandwichKernel uses this actual common kernel to prove annihilation by
arbitrary linear right factors ending in B_p, and vanishing of pair-sandwich
forms. Its operator-from-form consequence includes a bosonic-range condition,
which makes testing against the output legitimate. The concrete S3 and S4
kernel statements instead follow directly from the ambient common pair kernel.
The N=1 normalization in the identity W3-adjoint=B_p is checked explicitly:
both maps carry sqrt(3). No assumed coefficient matrix or desired gap
inequality is used to obtain these kernel statements.

CertificateRows defines actual one-body matrix-unit sums a_i-dagger a_j in
tensor coordinates. Rows lambda B_t + sum c(p,i,j) a_i-dagger a_j B_p therefore
annihilate the physical H kernel for arbitrary coefficients. Their Gram forms
are nonnegative for the elementary squared-norm reason. This establishes the
row class and kernel compatibility; it does not verify any proposed numerical
coefficient array, coefficient-dependent cancellation, or positive remainder.

2. Exact weight restriction and matrix action
--------------------------------------------
WeightBlocks proves weight conservation termwise using the support of the
pair vector: incoming and outgoing pair orbital sums agree. The distinct-slot
hypothesis is explicit for pairApply; the actual Hamiltonian always satisfies
it because its sum uses i<j. Weight projection commutes with H and with the
normalized bosonic symmetrizer.

WeightCoordinates gives mutually inverse restriction and zero extension
between the ordered weight-coordinate space and the ambient supported
subspace. H_block=R H I is accompanied by actual identities I H_block=H I
and H_block R=R H. The latter holds on every ambient vector because H cannot
mix the weight blocks. WeightIsBosonic is a separate condition, not silently
built into the configuration index type.

WeightBlockForms proves that I preserves the coordinate inner product exactly,
without an occupation multiplicity or factorial. The induced block bosonic
projector has bosonic range, the expected fixed points, idempotence and exact
self-adjointness. The matrix LinearMap.toMatrix'(H_block) is taken in the
ordered-configuration delta basis, acts by the actual H_block, and is Hermitian
and positive semidefinite in Mathlib's complex star order. These are exact
operator and matrix statements, rather than an asserted agreement of spectra.

3. Polynomial coordinates and finite pair matrices
-------------------------------------------------
PolynomialCoordinates multiplies an orthonormal tensor amplitude by
  S_Q(a)=product_i sqrt(binomial(Q,a_i)).
Every factor is strictly positive on the declared orbital range, including
Q=0, so this diagonal map is invertible. It preserves permutation symmetry.
The unweighted polynomial pair channel sums coefficients with x+y=p. Its
comparison with B_p contains the explicit nonzero factors sqrt(binomial(2Q,p))
and sqrt(choose(N+2,2)); therefore equality of common kernels is justified.

PolynomialMetric keeps the nonunitary character of this coordinate change
explicit. The inherited metric is diagonal with weight S_Q(a)^(-2), and the
transformed Hamiltonian is S_Q H S_Q^(-1), self-adjoint for that metric. An
ordinary unweighted coefficient Euclidean inner product is not substituted.
PolynomialWeightCoordinates proves the same coordinate relation after actual
weight restriction and links the finite physical H block kernel to the raw
polynomial pair equations on bosonic vectors.

PolynomialPairBlocks defines the channel d -> d-p by restricting the actual
polynomial channel. It proves that zero extension intertwines the maps and
that p>d gives zero. Thus natural-number subtraction at p>d hides no spurious
spectator-weight-zero contribution. Its rectangular matrices are exactly the
standard-coordinate matrices of those maps, and their common kernel detects
the actual transformed H kernel on bosonic inputs. They remain ordered tensor
blocks; no occupation-basis or highest-weight quotient is claimed.

PhysicalWeightMatrices composes these identities into a statement about the
actual physical H_block matrix kernel. The vector entering the raw pair
matrices is explicitly polynomialWeightCoordinates(phi), whereas phi enters
the orthonormal physical H matrix. This rescaling is retained in all four
operator/matrix variants of the theorem. Bose symmetry is required throughout.

4. Fixed-cap transport
----------------------
OrbitalCap proves every label in a total-deficit-d configuration is at most d.
For d<=Q it constructs inverse configuration and coefficient maps between
the weight-d coordinate sets with caps Q and d, preserving Bose symmetry.
OrbitalCapKernel transports the actual unweighted pair-channel sums. Terms
with a label above d vanish by support, and channels above their possible
orbital sums vanish. The Fin(2Q+1) and Fin(2d+1) index ranges are compared via
all natural channel indices, so no channel equations are dropped silently.
This is exact independence of polynomial common-kernel equations from the
cap in the stated range. It does not assert that physical H at different
fluxes has equal matrices, equal nonzero eigenvalues, or equal metrics.

WeightKernelTransport assembles the actual physical map
  T = D_d^(-1) cap D_Q,
where D_Q and D_d are the source and target polynomial-coordinate scalings.
It proves Bose symmetry in both directions and, on that symmetric subspace,
the equivalence of the actual source and target H_block zero conditions.
The submodule physicalWeightKernel is explicitly the intersection of the
pulled-back bosonic subspace with the actual block Hamiltonian kernel. The
restricted linear equivalence includes its inverse and proves equality of
kernel dimensions for every fixed N+2 and d with Q>=d. No rank-stability
assumption, dimension formula, or equality of nonzero spectra enters the
construction. The map's inverse uses the target normalization, so the
nonunitary rescaling is not silently discarded at this final step.

5. Boundaries and result strength
--------------------------------
The weight-coordinate statements quantify over all Q,N,d, including empty
weight blocks, N=0 and Q=0. The cap equivalence requires d<=Q explicitly.
Pair-annihilator and polynomial-pair results use total particle number N+2,
which is their natural domain. Sectors with zero or one particle are already
covered by the ambient pair-kernel theorem and hamiltonian_small_sector.

These modules provide exact model correspondence, kernel identities, conserved
finite blocks, and coordinate transports. They do not by themselves prove a
uniform positive gap or validate the research note's full certificate. The
occupation/highest-weight reduction and any flux-uniform inequalities still
require their own explicit maps and proofs.

Integration finding
-------------------
The review found one duplicate global declaration, configurationWeight_cons,
independently defined in PolynomialPairBlocks and OrbitalCapKernel. This is
an integration issue, not a mathematical discrepancy; it was reported to the
authors for resolution before the combined import/build. OrbitalCapKernel's
helper was renamed cap_configurationWeight_cons, its internal uses updated,
and its author reported a successful rebuild and source freeze. No scientific
normalization or hypothesis mismatch was found in the reviewed statements.
