OPTIMIZED SPHERICAL NEGATIVE TAIL: NO FLUX THRESHOLD IS NEEDED

THEOREM AND STATUS

In the full spherical bosonic V0 model with coefficient one per unordered
pair projector, define c9=30(5/9)^9=19531250/129140163 and nu=10^-10.
Then the following operator/form inequalities hold independently of total
particle number:

  T_{9,Q} <= (c9+nu)(S_{4,Q}+H_Q), EVERY integer Q>=0;              (1)
  T_{9,Q} <= (c9+nu-4/Q)(S_{4,Q}+H_Q), EVERY integer Q>=5000.       (2)

The same strengthened form holds with 4 replaced by 77/20 for every
Q>=2500, and with 4 replaced by 391/100 for every Q>=3000. These additional
options use the identical interval construction and are certified below.

This is an exact computer-assisted inequality. It uses the previously
derived exact spherical recoupling/eigenvalue and positive-lifting formulas.
The only new computer-assisted steps are finite rational arithmetic checks
specified below. The unbounded range of Q is certified by explicit rational
interval bounds on the entire interval 0<=1/Q<=1/256 (and <=1/5000 for
the improvement), NOT by checking finitely many large fluxes. The finite
remainder Q=9,...,255 is checked exhaustively. There is no floating-point
decision, fit, numerical spectral-gap input, or many-body cutoff.

1. A REAL COHERENT EXPONENT

Set a=2/27 and use M=aQ exactly, with no floor. The coherent auxiliary
operator A_{M,Q}, on V_Q tensor U_Q, is

  A_{M,Q}=(2Q+1)(Q+1) integral dOmega_alpha dOmega_beta
     s(alpha,beta)^M |alpha_pair><alpha_pair| tensor |beta><beta|,
  s(alpha,beta)=cos^2(theta_alpha,beta/2),                          (3)

with normalized sphere measures and normalized spin-coherent vectors.
This integral defines a positive rotation-invariant operator for EVERY
real M>=0; M is merely the exponent of a nonnegative scalar weight, not
a spin label or a dimension. The formulas formerly derived for integer
M hold for real M>=0: repeated integration by parts gives

  b_{j,Q}=(Q+1) binom(Q,j) integral_0^1 s^(M+Q-j)(1-s)^j ds
         =(Q+1)/(M+Q+1) * (Q)_j/(M+Q)_j.                        (4)

The integral converges for 0<=j<=Q and M>=0. Descending factorials of
the real number M+Q in (4) are positive, and the exact scalar marginal
is still (Q+1)/(M+1). Thus the exact positive lifting bound remains

  L3(W3 A_{M,Q} W3^dagger)
          <= [(Q+1)/(M+1)] (S4,Q+H_Q).                          (5)

Write Pi_z for the auxiliary spin-(3Q/2-z) projection, 0<=z<=Q, and
A_{M,Q}=sum_z tau_{z,Q} Pi_z. Recall the exact positive eigenvalue formula

  tau_{z,Q}=(2Q+1)/(3Q-2z+1) sum_{j=z}^Q b_{j,Q} gamma_{z,j,Q},
  gamma_{z,j,Q}=[(2Q)_z/(3Q-z+1)_z]
                 binom(j,z) (Q-z)_{j-z}/(3Q-2z)_{j-z},
  f_{z,Q}=(Q)_z/(2Q)_z.                                        (6)

Here (x)_k is a descending factorial with (x)_0=1. All factors in (6)
are positive on their relevant range, and tau_{z,Q}>0. The tail coefficient
is C_{9,Q}=2 sum_{odd z>=9} f_{z,Q} Pi_z.

The scalar absorption cost of spin z is

  k_{z,Q} := [(Q+1)/(M+1)] * [2 f_{z,Q}/tau_{z,Q}].              (7)

Consequently, bounding k_{z,Q} by a common K on the odd z>=9 blocks and
using the positive map X -> L3(W3 X W3^dagger) proves
T_{9,Q}<=K(S4,Q+H_Q). This includes the coherent marginal in the scalar
analysis; it must not be replaced by an uncontrolled asymptotic value.

2. A FINITE POSITIVE RATIONAL LOWER BOUND FOR THE RECIPROCAL COST

For Q>0 put x=1/Q. Define the reciprocal scalar

  D_{z,Q}:=[(M+1)/(Q+1)] tau_{z,Q}/f_{z,Q}=2/k_{z,Q}.             (8)

By dividing the j=z+n term of (6) by f_{z,Q} and cancelling powers of Q,

  D_{z,Q}=sum_{n=0}^{Q-z} g_{z,n}(x),                           (9)

where the following is an explicit rational function:

  g_{z,n}(x)=binom(z+n,z)
    [(2+x)(a+x)/((3-(2z-1)x)(1+a+x))]
    product_{i=0}^{z-1} [(2-i x)^2 /
                        ((1+a-i x)(3-(z-1+i)x))]
    product_{i=0}^{n-1} [(1-(z+i)x)^2 /
                        ((1+a-(z+i)x)(3-(2z+i)x))].             (10)

All terms in (9) are nonnegative. For Q>=256, z<=25, the fixed truncation

  G_z(x):=sum_{n=0}^{32} g_{z,n}(x) <= D_{z,Q}                  (11)

is valid because z+32<=57<Q. Every linear numerator and denominator
factor of G_z is strictly positive on 0<=x<=1/256.

3. ELEMENTARY EXACT INTERVAL CERTIFICATES

The interval checks are specified here completely, to make clear what the
companion script proves. Consider one rational term g(x)=b product_i(c_i+d_i x)
/product_j(e_j+f_j x), where b>0 and all linear factors are positive on
[0,h]. Repeated factors in (10), including squares, are listed repeatedly.
For any positive linear factor ell, let ell_- and ell_+ be the minimum
and maximum of its two endpoint values. Then

  g_-:=b product numerator_- / product denominator_+ <= g(x),
  g(x)<=g_+:=b product numerator_+ / product denominator_-.      (12)

Because d/(c+d x) has derivative -d^2/(c+d x)^2<=0,

  g'(x)/g(x) >= L_h:=sum_i d_i/(c_i+d_i h) - sum_j f_j/e_j.     (13)

Thus a rigorous constant lower bound for g' is

  beta_h(g):=L_h*g_- if L_h>=0;  L_h*g_+ if L_h<0.               (14)

All endpoint extrema, products, sums, and sign decisions in (12)--(14)
are rational. There is no discretization of [0,h]. Applying these formulas
to the precisely specified functions (10), exact integer/Fraction checks give

  sum_{n=0}^{32} beta_{1/256}(g_{9,n}) > 73;                    (15)
  G_9(0) >= 2/(c9+10^-10);                                    (16)
  sum_{n=0}^{32} (g_{z,n})_- > 20,
                      odd z in {11,13,...,25}, h=1/256.        (17)

For an independent expression checking the endpoint calculation,

  G_9(0)=(2/c9)(20/29)^10
                    sum_{n=0}^{32} binom(n+9,9)(9/29)^n.        (18)

The finite truncation raises 2/G_9(0) above c9 by approximately
4.58673e-11, strictly below 10^-10; the comparison in (16) is exact.
Equation (15) proves G_9(x)>=G_9(0) throughout the interval, so (11)
and (16) prove k_{9,Q}<=c9+nu for ALL Q>=256. Equation (17) proves
k_{z,Q}<1/10<c9 for the other retained odd z and all Q>=256.

4. ALL HIGH SPINS AND THE EXHAUSTIVE FINITE-FLUX REMAINDER

The earlier coarse all-z bound still holds when M=aQ is real:

  2 f_{z,Q}/tau_{z,Q} <= 3(1+a) rho^z, rho=3(1+a)/4=29/36.      (19)

Indeed it uses only M<=aQ, not integrality. It follows by keeping j=z
in (6) and bounding the exact product factors by rho, as in the previous
tail proof. Because a<1 and M=aQ exactly,

  (Q+1)/(M+1) <=1/a.                                          (20)

Consequently every z>=27, at every Q admitting that z, satisfies

  k_{z,Q} <= [3(1+a)/a](29/36)^27 <13/100<c9.                  (21)

Both scalar comparisons in (21) are verified rationally. For the finite
remaining range, the checker exhausts all 2151 pairs

  9<=Q<=255, Q integer; odd 9<=z<=min(Q,25),

and verifies the exact rational inequalities

  sum_{n=0}^{min(32,Q-z)} g_{z,n}(1/Q) >100/7.                 (22)

This positive partial sum is <=D_{z,Q}, so these blocks all have
k_{z,Q}<7/50<c9. The maximum certified upper bound among these finite
blocks occurs at Q=255,z=9 and is about 0.135684; no decimal is used in
the verification. For Q<9 there is no odd z>=9 in the auxiliary space,
so C_{9,Q}=T_{9,Q}=0 exactly. These statements exhaust ALL integers Q>=0
and all relevant z, proving (1).

5. A USEFUL NEGATIVE 1/Q CORRECTION

Apply exactly the same interval construction (12)--(14) on the smaller
interval h=1/5000. The exact checker additionally proves

  sum_{n=0}^{32} beta_{1/5000}(g_{9,n}) >353.                   (23)

Put C=c9+nu. From (16),(23), for Q>=5000,

  D_{9,Q}>=G_9(1/Q)>=2/C+353/Q,
  k_{9,Q}<=C/[1+353 C/(2Q)] <= C-4/Q.                        (24)

The last comparison follows by cross-multiplication with positive
denominators and the exact scalar inequality

  353 C^2 >= 4[2+353 C/5000].                                 (25)

The other z ranges satisfy k<1/10 or k<13/100, both strictly below
C-4/5000, again checked exactly. Thus the common scalar bound for ALL
odd z>=9 is C-4/Q whenever Q>=5000, proving (2).

More generally, if beta is a lower bound for G_9' on [0,1/Q_*], the same
argument gives the improvement C-d/Q for Q>=Q_* whenever

  beta C^2 >= d(2+beta C/Q_*),  C-d/Q_*>13/100.                  (26)

The checker additionally verifies the exact triples

  (Q_*, beta, d) = (2500, 681/2, 77/20),
                   (3000, 345, 391/100),
                   (5000, 353, 4).

Thus, in particular, ALL Q>=3000 admit d=391/100. These are independent
uniform interval certificates, not interpolation between sampled fluxes.

6. REPLAY AND OUTPUTS

Run, from any directory,

  python -S /absolute/path/to/optimized_tail_threshold_checker.py

The checker uses only fractions.Fraction, math, pathlib, and json. It
fails with a nonzero exit status if any required inequality fails. It
writes optimized_tail_threshold_results.json next to itself, containing
the exact rational derivative lower bounds, endpoint values and margins,
eight remaining low-z interval bounds, the coarse tail bound, and the
finite exhaustion count and worst block. The definitions of the rational
functions and all interval operations are included in the script.

The LaTeX fragment optimized_tail_threshold.tex gives the same proof for
the manuscript appendix. This result supersedes the earlier conservative
Q>=10^7 negative-tail threshold; the improved auxiliary kernel is allowed
to have a real coherent exponent. The physical Hamiltonian and tail
operator are unchanged.
