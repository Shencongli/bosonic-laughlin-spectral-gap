# Lean formalization: operator bounds and occupation/highest-weight coordinates

This package proves the coherent occupation bound, its pair-annihilation
lift, and the normal-order decomposition for the repository's full spherical
bosonic V0 model. It also constructs the three-particle auxiliary map,
proves its Gram and Hamiltonian identities, and gives the complete spectrum
and projector ranks of the actual compressed exchange. The physical common
pair kernel, exact weight blocks, and their polynomial-coordinate transport
are formalized as well. The current extension supplies complete occupation
and highest-weight coordinates, including the physical normalization,
spherical metric, integer annihilation matrices, and exact Hamiltonian form.
**The uniform spectral-gap theorem remains unproved in Lean.**

Prepared and curated by Xin Shen. The Lean code and this account of its status
were generated by OpenAI Codex on 7–8 October 2026 under Xin Shen's direction.
No independent human audit is claimed.

## Model

`Orbital Q = Fin (Q+1)` labels the orthonormal lowest-Landau-level orbitals at
integer sphere flux Q. `State Q N` is the complex tensor-coordinate space
`(Fin N → Fin (Q+1)) → ℂ`. The physical sector is the complex submodule
`bosonicSubspace Q N` of permutation-invariant vectors. It represents
`Sym^N U_Q` without an occupation-number cutoff.

`pairVector` uses the coefficient

$$t_p(i,j)=\delta_{i+j,p}\sqrt{\binom Qi\binom Qj/\binom{2Q}p}.$$

`hamiltonian` sums its rank-one pair kernels over all p and all unordered
tensor slots i<j, with pair coefficient one. `hamiltonianLinear` packages
this formula as a complex linear map on the ambient tensor space.

The pair operators are proved idempotent and Hermitian, and the Hamiltonian
is proved positive, self-adjoint, and invariant on the bosonic subspace.
All these results hold at arbitrary Q and N.
On the bosonic sector, `FockCorrespondence.lean` also proves the exact
quadratic-form identity $\langle\psi,H_Q\psi\rangle=\sum_p\|B_p\psi\|^2$,
with the manuscript's normalized pair coefficients and the factor
$\sqrt{\binom N2}$ in the contraction map.

## Proved occupation bound

A normalized complex spinor `(u,v)`, with |u|²+|v|²=1, defines the
spin-coherent orbital

$$\beta_i=\sqrt{\binom Qi}\,u^{Q-i}v^i,\qquad 0\le i\le Q.$$

The code proves its normalization and its exact double-orbital expansion
in the V0 pair space. If $P_{\beta,i}$ denotes the rank-one orbital projector
in particle slot i, the number operator is
$n_\beta=\sum_iP_{\beta,i}$. For normalized beta, the commuting-projector
identity gives

$$n_\beta^2-n_\beta=2\sum_{i<j}P_{\beta,i}P_{\beta,j}.$$

The checked results then give, as quadratic-form inequalities,

$$\frac12n_\beta(n_\beta-I)\le H_Q,\qquad n_\beta\le H_Q+I.$$

These hold for every Q and N, including the vacuum sector; no large-flux
threshold or particle-number cutoff is used. On symmetric tensor states,
the number form is also proved equal to the squared norm of the usual
annihilation map $a_\beta$, with its $\sqrt N$ normalization.

`pairAnnihilate` is the linear map from N+2 particles to N with coefficient
$\sqrt{\binom{N+2}{2}}$. It preserves bosonic symmetry and is proved equal
to $B_\alpha=a_\alpha^2/\sqrt2$. The main lifted result is

$$\langle B_\alpha\psi,n_\beta B_\alpha\psi\rangle
\le\langle B_\alpha\psi,H_{Q,N}B_\alpha\psi\rangle
+\|B_\alpha\psi\|^2.$$

Here psi has N+2 particles, both operators on the right act in the output
N-particle sector, beta is normalized and spin-coherent, and alpha may be
any orbital. This is the quadratic-form version of the manuscript's
$B_\alpha^\dagger n_\beta B_\alpha$ inequality.

The statements quantify over finite-dimensional sectors. Construction of
unbounded operators and their domains on the completed Fock direct sum is
not included in this package.

## Normal ordering and the three-particle map

For the same pair projectors $P_{ij}$ that define $H_Q$, the code defines
$S_3$ as the sum of $P_{ij}P_{kl}$ over ordered choices of two distinct,
overlapping unordered pairs, and $S_4$ as the sum over ordered choices of two disjoint unordered
pairs. It then proves, for every Q and N on the ambient tensor space,

$$H_Q^2=H_Q+S_3+S_4,\qquad S_4\ge0.$$

The two terms are Hermitian. Their definitions are explicit sums of pair
products, independent of this identity. On bosonic sectors with at least
four particles, the code also proves the exact quadratic-form identity

$$\langle\psi,S_4\psi\rangle=\sum_{p,q}\|B_qB_p\psi\|^2.$$

The factor $\binom N2\binom{N-2}2$ is derived both from disjoint-pair counting
and from the two normalized pair-annihilation maps. On sectors with fewer
than four particles, the code proves $S_4=0$ directly.

Combining this correspondence with the occupation bound gives, for every
bosonic input sector with at least two particles,

$$\sum_p\langle B_p\psi,n_\beta B_p\psi\rangle
\le\langle\psi,S_4\psi\rangle+\langle\psi,H_Q\psi\rangle.$$

Here beta is any normalized spin-coherent orbital, and the number operator
acts in the sector after pair annihilation. The bound has no factor growing
with particle number.

The auxiliary space `ThreeBodyAux Q` has coordinates $(p,k)$, where
$0\le p\le2Q$ labels the normalized pair-spin-Q basis and $0\le k\le Q$
labels the remaining orbital. It represents $V_Q\otimes U_Q$.
`threeBodyMap` is the map $W_3$ into the symmetric three-particle space:

$$ (W_3\phi)(a,b,c)=\frac1{\sqrt3}\sum_p
\big[t_p(a,b)\phi(p,c)+t_p(a,c)\phi(p,b)+t_p(b,c)\phi(p,a)\big]. $$

`threeBodyAdjoint` is proved to be its adjoint when its input is bosonic.
The explicit contraction map `compressedSwap` is

$$ (T\phi)(p,k)=\sum_{x,y,q}t_p(x,y)t_q(x,k)\phi(q,y). $$

The checked identities are

$$W_3^\dagger W_3=I+2T,\qquad W_3W_3^\dagger=H_{Q,3},\qquad
S_{3,Q,3}=2W_3TW_3^\dagger.$$

The last two identities act on the bosonic three-particle space. The lift
to arbitrary particle number is described below.

## Complete recoupling spectrum

For $0\le z\le Q$, let

$$f_{z,Q}=\frac{(Q)_z}{(2Q)_z},\qquad \lambda_z=(-1)^zf_{z,Q},$$

where $(a)_z=a(a-1)\cdots(a-z+1)$ and $(a)_0=1$. The index z labels the
highest-weight total orbital deficit p+k. The code proves

$$\operatorname{spec}(T)=\{\lambda_z:0\le z\le Q\},\qquad
T=\sum_{z=0}^Q\lambda_z\Pi_z,\qquad
\sum_{z=0}^Q\Pi_z=I.$$

Here $\Pi_z$ is the orthogonal spectral projector on the auxiliary space
$V_Q\otimes U_Q$, with dimensions $2Q+1$ and $Q+1$ for the two factors.
The projectors are proved Hermitian, idempotent, and mutually orthogonal,
and their ranges are the actual eigenspaces of T. Their exact ranks are

$$\operatorname{rank}\Pi_z=3Q-2z+1.$$

The proof uses the concrete raising, lowering, and weight operators and
their $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ commutators. At each z it constructs a nonzero
highest-weight vector whose magnetic weight is $(3Q-2z)/2$. Applying the
lowering operator n times, for $0\le n\le3Q-2z$, gives nonzero,
linearly independent descendants.
These give eigenspace dimension bounds; their sum equals the full auxiliary
dimension $(2Q+1)(Q+1)$, which fixes every multiplicity. Hermitian-matrix
diagonalization supplies a complete eigenbasis. The coefficient identities
in `Recoupling.lean`, including $f_{z,Q}\le2^{-z}$, are thus connected to the
actual contraction map.

The resulting Gram and three-particle normal-order decompositions are

$$W_3^\dagger W_3=\sum_{z=0}^Q(1+2\lambda_z)\Pi_z,\qquad
S_{3,Q,3}=\sum_{z=0}^Q2\lambda_z W_3\Pi_zW_3^\dagger.$$

The second identity acts on bosonic three-particle states.

For every Q>0, the code also constructs the nonzero auxiliary vector
$\phi=|p=0,k=1\rangle-\sqrt{1/2}|p=1,k=0\rangle$ and proves
$W_3\phi=0$ and $T\phi=-\phi/2$. The complete z=1 auxiliary eigenspace
has dimension $3Q-1$ and vanishing Gram eigenvalue.

## Three-body lift in every finite sector

Let M be the total particle number, and let $P_M$ be the orthogonal
projection from the ordered tensor space $U_Q^{\otimes M}$ to
$\operatorname{Sym}^M U_Q$. `bosonicProjection` defines $P_M$ by averaging
all particle permutations with coefficient $1/M!$ and proves its
idempotence, self-adjointness, and range.

For a complex linear map A on the three-particle tensor space, the lift is

$$\mathcal L_3^{(M)}(A)=\binom M3P_M(A\otimes I_{M-3})P_M
\quad(M\ge3),\qquad \mathcal L_3^{(M)}(A)=0\quad(M<3).$$

Here A acts on the first three tensor slots. The code constructs their
slices explicitly and proves that slices of a bosonic state are bosonic.
The lift depends only on A's action on symmetric three-particle inputs.
It preserves self-adjointness, positive quadratic forms, and their ordering.
Its identity normalization on the physical sector is
$\mathcal L_3^{(M)}(I)=\binom M3I$.

The pair-product counting and slice identities prove, for every Q and M
on the bosonic sector,

$$S_{3,Q,M}=\mathcal L_3^{(M)}(S_{3,Q,3}).$$

Write $K_z=W_3\Pi_zW_3^\dagger$ for the three-particle coefficient of
the z-th auxiliary spectral block. The all-particle formulas are

$$S_{3,Q,M}=\sum_{z=0}^Q2\lambda_z\mathcal L_3^{(M)}(K_z),\qquad
H_{Q,M}^2=H_{Q,M}+\sum_{z=0}^Q2\lambda_z\mathcal L_3^{(M)}(K_z)+S_{4,Q,M}.$$

Each lifted block $\mathcal L_3^{(M)}(K_z)$ is proved Hermitian and positive.

The connection to the manuscript's normal-order convention is also checked.
For $M\ge3$ and a three-particle vector w, the annihilation map on a physical
M-particle state is

$$\big(A_M(w)\psi\big)(a)=\sqrt{\binom M3}\,\langle w,\psi_a\rangle,$$

where $\psi_a$ is the three-slot slice obtained by fixing the remaining
M−3 orbital labels a. The code defines its creation adjoint explicitly and
proves

$$\mathcal L_3^{(M)}(|w\rangle\langle w'|)=A_M(w)^\dagger A_M(w'),
\qquad A_3(w)^\dagger|\mathrm{vac}\rangle=w$$

with the vacuum identity for bosonic w.

## Physical kernels and exact weight blocks

For bosonic states with M≥2 particles, the code proves

$$\langle\psi,H_{Q,M}\psi\rangle=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_{Q,M}\psi=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B_p\psi=0\ \text{for every }0\le p\le2Q.$$

On the ambient tensor space, at every particle number, the Hamiltonian
kernel is also proved to be the common kernel of all distinct-slot pair
projectors. This implies that the actual S3 and S4 operators annihilate it.
On three bosonic particles, the adjoint $W_3^\dagger$ and every coefficient
$W_3 A W_3^\dagger$ vanish on that kernel as well.

The total orbital deficit of an ordered configuration a is
$d(a)=\sum_{i=1}^M a_i$. `WeightState Q M d` is the coefficient space on
configurations with $d(a)=d$; Bose symmetry is imposed separately.
Restriction and zero extension give an exact linear identification with
the supported subspace. The pair projectors, Hamiltonian, and bosonic
projection preserve these blocks. Their finite Hamiltonian matrices, in
the delta basis of ordered configurations, are proved Hermitian and positive
semidefinite and represent the actual restricted operator.

To express the pair equations without spherical binomial factors, define
the invertible diagonal coordinate map

$$ (D_Q\psi)(a)=s_Q(a)\psi(a),\qquad
s_Q(a)=\prod_{i=1}^M\sqrt{\binom Q{a_i}}. $$

This map preserves Bose symmetry and total deficit. It is nonunitary in
the original coordinate inner product. The induced polynomial-coordinate
metric and transformed Hamiltonian are

$$\langle\chi,\eta\rangle_{D_Q}
=\sum_a\frac{\overline{\chi(a)}\eta(a)}{s_Q(a)^2},\qquad
\widetilde H_{Q,M}=D_QH_{Q,M}D_Q^{-1}.$$

The metric is proved positive definite, and $\widetilde H$ is Hermitian
in this metric. For M=N+2, the unweighted polynomial pair channel is the map
from M to N tensor slots defined by

$$ (C_p\chi)(a)=\sum_{x,y=0}^Q\mathbf1_{x+y=p}\chi(x,y,a). $$

Its exact correspondence to the normalized physical pair map is

$$\sqrt{\binom M2}\,C_pD_Q^{(M)}
=\sqrt{\binom{2Q}p}\,D_Q^{(M-2)}B_p.$$

Here the superscript specifies the particle sector of D. The code constructs
the finite channel matrices from deficit d to d−p, proves vanishing for
p>d, and identifies their common kernel with the physical Hamiltonian
kernel after the coordinate change and Bose restriction.

Every orbital label in a configuration of deficit d is at most d. For
Q≥d, this gives an explicit bijection between the configurations at caps
Q and d and a linear equivalence of their coefficient spaces. The equivalence
preserves Bose symmetry and the common polynomial pair kernel, with the
different channel-index ranges treated explicitly. Composing it with
$D_Q$ and $D_d^{-1}$ gives an explicit linear equivalence of the physical
zero spaces for M≥2. At fixed M and d, their dimensions therefore agree
for every Q≥d. This is a kernel identification; the induced metrics and the
nonzero Hamiltonian spectra are not identified across fluxes.

The code also defines the certificate row class
$R=cB_t+\sum_{p,i,j}c_{pij}a_i^\dagger a_jB_p$ in tensor coordinates,
with arbitrary complex coefficients, and proves that its rows and Gram
forms vanish on the physical kernel.

## Occupation and highest-weight coordinate bridge

The 8 October 2026 extension uses occupations A that are multisets of N
orbital labels in $\{0,\ldots,Q\}$. Their multiplicities are $n_j(A)$,
and their total degree is $d=\sum_j j n_j(A)$. The occupation space at fixed
N and d has one coefficient $c_A$ per such multiset. A proved bijection
identifies these labels with nondecreasing orbital tuples; when Q≥d,
the orbital cap is inactive. This is the mathematical index set used by
the verifier's `parts(n,D)`.

Let $F_A=\prod_j n_j(A)!$. The polynomial occupation inclusion assigns
$F_Ac_A$ to every ordered tensor configuration with occupation A. Its
factorial diagonal is invertible, and the inclusion is proved to cover
the full bosonic space. For Q>0 the actual physical coordinate map is

$$\Phi_{Q,N,d}(c)=\sqrt{\frac{Q^d}{N!}}\,
D_Q^{-1}I_{\mathrm{occ}}c.$$

Here $I_{\mathrm{occ}}$ is this factorial-weighted inclusion, with zero
extension outside degree d. The map is injective and onto the bosonic
degree-d sector. The sector scalar and the inverse spherical diagonal
are both part of its definition.

The occupation pair matrix $\widehat B_{p,N,d}$ maps N particles of
degree d to N−2 particles of degree d−p. It sums successive removals
of labels x and y with x+y=p. Each ordered pair (x,y) contributes
$n_x(A)n_y(A\setminus\{x\})$ from A to $A\setminus\{x,y\}$; repeated labels
therefore contribute $n_x(A)(n_x(A)-1)$. These integer coefficients are
proved from the actual polynomial pair contraction. Channels with p>d
vanish. The resulting common matrix kernel is exactly the physical
Hamiltonian kernel under $\Phi$.

The nonorthonormal metric is also derived from the physical inner product.
Define

$$P_{j,Q}=\prod_{\ell=0}^{j-1}(1-\ell/Q),\qquad
E_{p,Q}=\prod_{\ell=0}^{p-1}(1-\ell/(2Q)),\qquad
\kappa_p^2=\frac{p!}{2^{p+1}}.$$

Empty products are one. The diagonal metric on the occupation coefficients is

$$G_{Q,N,d}(A,A)=
\frac{\prod_j n_j(A)!\,(j!)^{n_j(A)}}{\prod_j P_{j,Q}^{n_j(A)}}.$$

For Q>0 and N≥2, the checked Hamiltonian form is

$$\langle\Phi_{Q,N,d}c,H_Q\Phi_{Q,N,d}e\rangle=
\sum_{p=0}^{\min(2Q,d)}\frac{\kappa_p^2}{E_{p,Q}}
(\widehat B_{p,N,d}c)^*G_{Q,N-2,d-p}(\widehat B_{p,N,d}e).$$

The matrix $F_{AB}=\langle\Phi e_A,H_Q\Phi e_B\rangle$ has the
sesquilinear-form formula implemented by `optimized_head_interval.py`
`H()`. The operator-action matrix in these coordinates is $G^{-1}F$.
Both the metric and the pair prefactors above agree with the finite-sphere
formulas used by the verifier.

The center-of-mass derivative matrix $C_{N,d}$ replaces one source label
j by j−1 with coefficient $j n_j(A)$. Its entries, including the integer
multiplicity, are derived from the tensor raising operator. With the
degree-dependent normalization above, for d>0,

$$J_+\Phi_{Q,N,d}=\sqrt Q\,\Phi_{Q,N,d-1}C_{N,d}.$$

For Q>0, $\ker C_{N,d}$ therefore represents the complete physical
highest-weight space at degree d. This includes a surjectivity proof,
independent of any proposed numerical nullspace frame. For d>0 and Q≥d,
the entry formula matches `cm_lower(n,d)` under the sorted-tuple indexing.
At d=0 both maps are zero: Lean uses a degree-zero target and the script an
empty target, so their kernels agree despite the different target spaces.

`KernelFrames.lean` supplies an exact criterion for a proposed frame U
to be a full kernel basis: matrices L and V must satisfy
$CU=0$, $LU=I$, and $UL+VC=I$. The criterion gives a linear equivalence
with the kernel and its dimension. Applying it to the retained rational
arrays now verifies all 26 retained frames: N=3 with d=0,…,8 and N=4 with
d=0,…,16. The rational matrices are represented by sparse integer rows and
common denominators. Lean checks the integer identities obtained by clearing
those denominators, then proves $CU=0$, $LU=I$, and $UL+VC=I$ over the
rationals. Extending scalars to the complex numbers preserves the identities.

The index correspondence is checked as well. A recursive partition
enumeration is proved to contain every nondecreasing tuple of length N and
sum d exactly once. Each exported table is checked against that enumeration,
and every CM entry is checked against the integer occupation formula.
For Q≥d>0, this identifies the retained C with the actual spherical CM
matrix in that table order. The columns of U then give a complete basis
of the physical bosonic highest-weight space after factorial occupation
inclusion and inverse spherical scaling. The code supplies the explicit
linear equivalence, its action on coefficients, and the resulting dimension.
These bases are generally nonorthogonal. The displayed `physical_apply` maps
use $D_Q^{-1}I_{\mathrm{occ}}U$. Multiplying by $\sqrt{Q^d/N!}$ gives the
$\Phi$ convention and its metric above. At d=0, the retained column is [1], and a separate proof
gives the full one-dimensional highest-weight space for every Q, including
Q=0, without identifying the two different CM target spaces.

The frame checks establish basis completeness. The full comparison matrices
and their finite or interval positivity certificates still require proofs.

## Gap target

`UniformGapTarget` states the requested estimate for all integer Q≥3088,
all N, and all bosonic states psi:

$$\tfrac13\langle\psi,H_Q\psi\rangle\le\|H_Q\psi\|^2.$$

It remains a **definition of a proposition**, not a theorem or an assumed axiom.
At Q=2(N−1), the final task also includes identification of the Laughlin kernel
and deduction of the gap above it.

## Remaining proof chain

1. Identify the full comparison matrices with the exact occupation-coordinate
   operators. The coordinate map, metric, pair matrices, and retained
   highest-weight bases are now proved.
2. Prove the two-spectator bound, coherent integrals and comparisons,
   sphere transfer, and Schur averaging. The occupation sandwich above is
   one input to these further estimates.
3. Derive the certificate matrices from the operators and verify all exact
   and interval certificates, including their remainders, inside Lean.
4. Assemble the six flux ranges into `UniformGapTarget`, then identify the
   Laughlin zero mode and derive the spectral statement.

The existing Python success flags are not imported as Lean hypotheses.

## Reproduction

Install Lean using the pinned `lean-toolchain`, then from this directory run:

```sh
lake exe cache get
python check.py
```

`check.py` runs `lake build`, checks the physical statements in
`StatementChecks.lean`, invokes Lean on `Audit.lean`, and rejects
axiom dependencies outside `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.
It also checks that every theorem declaration is listed in the audit.
It writes `verification-local.json`, which records checks of this package,
not verification of the manuscript's main theorem.

Lean is pinned to v4.34.1 and mathlib to
`d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612`; transitive revisions are locked
in `lake-manifest.json`. The initial Windows build used source archives of
these same revisions through Lake's package overrides because Git transport
was unavailable; the archived build record states this explicitly.

## Sources

The formalization strategy is informed by OpenAI's
[fermionic Fock-space formalization](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/OAI/Analysis/LaughlinFock/Main.lean)
and [spherical gap formalization](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/OAI/Analysis/LaughlinGap/Main.lean).
Their fermionic theorems are not used as bosonic theorems here. This package
does not copy their solution modules and does not claim equivalent coverage.

The coefficient normalization uses mathlib's `Nat.add_choose_eq`
([Vandermonde source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612/Mathlib/Data/Nat/Choose/Vandermonde.lean),
attributed there to Johan Commelin). The physical model and earlier research
references are given in the manuscript and repository README.

The complete eigenbasis uses mathlib's Hermitian-matrix spectral theorem
in [`Mathlib/Analysis/Matrix/Spectrum.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612/Mathlib/Analysis/Matrix/Spectrum.lean),
credited there to Alexander Bentkamp. The file reduces matrix diagonalization
to mathlib's inner-product-space spectral theorem. The recoupling eigenvalues,
their connection to the concrete pair contraction, and their multiplicities
are proved in this package using those library results.
